About the data

A note on the numbers.

Small areas. Important uncertainty.

These are survey estimates, not exact counts. Select an area to see its margin of error. Differences in color do not establish statistically significant differences.

Sources: City of Chicago, community areas, and Census TIGER/Line. Rent and housing counts: ACS 2010–2014, 2015–2019, and 2020–2024 (B25058, B25064, B25063, B25003, B25070), matched to each release’s TIGER boundaries. Rents are in each period’s end-year dollars, without adjustment for inflation. Boundaries can change between periods. Colors show estimates, not statistical significance. Whole block groups and tracts with positive city overlap are shown; some extend beyond city limits.

Reading margins of error

The ± amount next to an estimate is its Census-published margin of error at the 90% confidence level. These describe sampling uncertainty; they do not include all possible sources of error.

Rent burden and renter share

Renter-occupied share is the percentage of occupied homes that are rented: renter-occupied units divided by total occupied housing units (ACS table B25003). It includes homes occupied without cash rent and excludes vacant homes, including vacant rentals.

The 30% or more and 50% or more rent-burden measures show the shares spending at least those percentages of household income on gross rent. Both denominators exclude units for which the rent/income ratio could not be computed.

Percentage margins of error are approximate and shown in percentage points (pp), using Census-style formulas for sums and subset proportions. Missing inputs and zero denominators remain unavailable, rather than becoming zero percentages. Citywide percentages are calculated from citywide counts, not by averaging mapped areas.

Model variables and violent crime

The additional housing, renter-profile, poverty, income, education and racial/ethnic measures cover 2010–2014, 2015–2019 and 2020–2024. Tract and block-group values use their own published ACS counts and margins of error. Chicago dashboard values come directly from the Chicago city place—not averages of small areas. Household income is shown in annual dollars, although it enters the regression in logs.

ACS measures can be compared across the peer cities for all three periods. Some racial-composition source totals are controlled to fixed population values. Following Census guidance, their sampling margins of error are treated as zero when calculating derived shares; original annotations are retained.

Housing and renter-profile shares use renter-occupied units or households. Bedroom categories include studio/one bedroom, exactly two bedrooms and three or more bedrooms. The one-unit-building rental share combines detached and attached homes, such as row houses. Education shares use adults age 25+, poverty uses the population with poverty status determined, and racial/ethnic shares use all residents. Recent moves means the householder moved into the unit in 2010+, 2015+ or 2020+, respectively. These are period-specific cutoffs, not an annual turnover rate.

Violent crime counts use frozen extracts of revised historical CPD 2010–2024 records and the documented IUCR definition: Index murder, criminal sexual assault, robbery and aggravated assault/battery. Each row counts once, including separate murder-victim rows. The map counts usable geocoded rows within whole Census areas; the citywide total includes missing-coordinate rows. Zero assigned rows does not establish no violence. These are raw counts, not rates or resident risk, and no survey margin of error applies. The annual chart uses single-year CPD counts rather than five-year ACS estimates.

Community-area outlines and popup names show the location controls used in the model. Whole block groups can cross community and city boundaries; the block-group model assignment uses the largest community overlap. City-comparison popups remain available for the original dashboard measures; new model-measure cards are Chicago-only.
